Seat View Studio · Manual

Quick start

  1. Start the app with npm run dev (or open the built dist/ through any local web server). Opening index.html directly from disk does not work.
  2. Load the scene. Drop the COLMAP sparse folder and the splat .ply on the viewport, together or one after the other. The Scene panel turns green for Cameras and Splat.
  3. Set the target (required). Hold Shift, click the pitch and drag up to raise the point. Every section will look at it. The export does not start without it.
  4. Check the Output settings: width (height is always half), FOV, and the height / right offsets.
  5. Start export session. The viewport now shows exactly the image that will be saved, starting at section 1.
  6. Fast way: press Export all. Every section is rendered with the offsets, then the Review sheet opens. Click any section that looks wrong, fix it and press R.
  7. By hand: for each section, fine-tune if needed and press R to save it and go to the next one. Use ← / → to go back or skip, N for the next section without an image.
  8. Download ZIP (in the review, or Download in the panel) saves the images plus camera.json. Drop that ZIP again next time to get the same target and offsets back.

Press H or the Manual button in the bottom-right corner any time to open this manual again.

Speed

The base speed is 3% of the scene size per second, so a big stadium and a small room both feel slow and controllable. The Fly speed slider (or the wheel) multiplies it from 0.01× to 100×. The panel shows the result in scene units per second (metres, if your COLMAP model is in metres). The speed is remembered the next time you open the page.

Also

Double-click centers the view on the point under the cursor.

Export session

  • Sections are the COLMAP cameras with _center in the image name, in file order. The PNG for a section is named after the camera without _center and the extension, so section_12_center.jpg → section_12.png.
  • What is saved: a full-frame equidistant fisheye, 2:1, at the Output width. The FOV spans the image width. The viewport is the export, so what you see is what you get. Overlays (section label, target stem, text) are never saved.
  • How a section is framed: the camera goes to the section camera's position. It looks at the target if one is set, otherwise it keeps the COLMAP orientation. Then the Right offset moves it sideways along its own right direction and Height moves it straight up, and it re-aims at the target.
  • Manual adjustments: you can also fly or look around before pressing R; R saves the current view. U throws away your manual move and returns to the computed section pose. Changing an offset, the FOV or the target also re-frames the section.
  • Saving again: the session line says "this section saved". Press R again on it to replace the image.
  • Export all renders every section with its offsets, one after the other, in a few seconds. Sections you saved by hand with R are kept as they are; auto-rendered ones are rendered again, so after changing an offset just press Export all again. Esc or Stop ends it early. The panel is locked while it runs.
  • Next unsaved (N) jumps to the next section without an image, wrapping around.
  • Review shows every section as a thumbnail with a badge: auto (Export all), by hand (R) and own offsets. Missing ones are striped. Click a tile to go to that section. It also opens on its own after Export all and after saving the last section.
  • Camera file: camera.json holds the target, width, FOV, level horizon and all offsets (global and per section). It has no camera positions, splat or images: those still come from the files you drop. It is inside every ZIP; Export camera (Scene panel, any time, also in the review) downloads it on its own. Import camera or dropping the JSON or the ZIP on the viewport restores everything, before or after loading the scene, also during a session.
  • Copy image (B, or the button in the bottom bar) renders the current view and puts it on the clipboard, so you can paste it into a chat or an AI tool with Ctrl+V. In a session it is exactly the export image (same size, FOV and fisheye); outside a session it is the viewport as you see it. Guides are never in it.
  • Ending: Download downloads what is saved (plus camera.json) and ends the session. Cancel ends it and discards the images (it asks first). The width is locked while a session runs.

Target & offsets

Target

  • Required before exporting. Until it is set, the Target row is orange and Start export session only reminds you how to set it.
  • Hold Shift, press the left button on visible ground (there must be splats under the cursor), move the mouse up: the top of the dashed stem stays under the cursor, so the target is where you point. Release to set it.
  • A dashed stem shows the height while you drag. The Scene panel then shows "set" with the height.
  • Setting a target during a session re-aims the current section right away. ✕ next to it clears it, and sections go back to their COLMAP orientation.

Level horizon

  • Off (default): each section keeps the slight sideways tilt of its COLMAP camera, exactly as before. Looking around with the mouse keeps that tilt.
  • On: sections and Focus camera use a perfectly level horizon. Changing it during a session re-frames the current section.

Offsets

  • Height moves the eye straight up (negative = down). Right moves it sideways (negative = left). Forward moves it level toward where the section looks (negative = back).
  • The step is 1% of the drawn target height, or 1% of the scene size without a target. The current step is shown under the fields.
  • Change them with the − / + buttons, I/K (height) and J/L (right), O/; (forward). Hold Shift for 10 steps at once. You can also type a value.
  • Offsets stay the same for all following sections until you change them.
  • This section only: tick it during a session to give the current section its own Height / Right / Forward. The fields turn amber and the section's dot on the mini-map is amber. All other sections keep the global offsets. Untick it to send the section back to the global ones. Own offsets are kept by section name, also in camera.json.
  • Use camera position (P): fly the camera freely to the spot you want, then press it. Height, Right and Forward are set to where the camera is now, relative to its section (the current section in a session, otherwise the nearest one), so every following section gets the same move. Only the position is taken: the camera is still aimed at the target, so where you looked does not matter.

Guides

Guides are drawn on a separate layer above the image. They are never in the exported PNGs, so leave them on while you work. Switch them with the Show guides button or G. Your choice is remembered.

Section dots & names

  • A green dot marks every _center camera, with its section name next to it (the image name without _center).
  • The yellow dot with a ring is the reference section: the current section during an export session, otherwise the section nearest to you.
  • Click a dot or its name to jump to that section. During an export session this switches the session to that section (with your offsets), like pressing ← / → several times. Outside a session ← / → also step through the sections.
  • When names would overlap, the nearer ones win and the others are hidden until you get closer.

Target line

  • While a target is set (Shift + drag), a yellow line always runs from the centre of the view (small cross) to the target point.
  • When the cross sits on the target, you are looking straight at it. If the target is outside the view, the line runs to the edge and ends in an arrow pointing toward it.

Focus camera

  • The Focus camera button under the mini-map (or V) turns the camera, without moving it, so it looks exactly at the target. The target then sits on the centre cross.
  • Handy after flying or looking around: one click and you are aimed again. During a session it uses the same horizon as the section framing.

Mini-map (top right)

  • During a session it shows your progress: a solid dot is saved, a ring still needs an image, amber has its own offsets.
  • A top-down map of all sections. The small red dot is your camera; the dashed yellow line goes back to the reference section.
  • The map keeps the same framing while you move. If you fly outside the section area the red dot stays pinned to the edge.

Offset readout (under the map)

  • How far the camera is from the reference section, in that section's own directions: R right (negative = left), F forward and H height, in the same directions as the offsets, plus the straight distance.
  • With only a Right offset set, you should see that value after R and close to 0 for F and H. Use camera position copies exactly these numbers into the offsets.
  • If another section is closer than the reference section, the box turns red and says closer to …. That usually means the offset pushed you into the next section.

Troubleshooting

The keys do nothing
A number field still has the keyboard. Press Enter or Esc, or click the viewport.
"Start export session" does not start
It needs the cameras (with _center names), the splat and the target. The Scene panel shows which one is missing; the message tells you what to do.
"JSZip did not load"
The ZIP library comes from the internet (cdnjs). Connect and reload the page.
"No splat under the cursor" when setting the target
Shift-click on ground that has splats, not on the background.
Moving is too fast or too slow
Turn the mouse wheel in game mode, or use the Fly speed slider. Hold X for fine moves.
The point cloud and frustums are not shown
Normal when the cameras are loaded with the splat: only their poses are needed.
The page stays blank or says it cannot load
Serve the folder over HTTP (see Quick start). Browsers block the engine when the file is opened directly.
